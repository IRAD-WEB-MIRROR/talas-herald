Nurzhan's air needs a source count, a gas date and a refuse figure

By The Herald's View in Opinion on 21 November 2026 | 06:43 ✎ 0

Share: TulparE-mailPrint

A reading of 196 is a warning. Without a published list of what is burning, it is not yet a plan.

Nurzhan's air reached 196 on the US scale this week, in the unhealthy band, with fine particles at 119 micrograms per cubic metre. That is roughly eight times the 15 that the World Health Organization recommends as a daily limit. Talas read 106 and Iyık Janyl 141 at the same time. For some 850,000 people in the Zone's second city, the heating season has come with a taste in the air at six in the morning, as one coach driver told the Herald, and a street whose far end disappears.

The official response so far has been to advise residents to take precautions during the inversion. That is sensible advice for a bad week. It is not an answer to the question residents are asking, which is what they are breathing and who is responsible for it. Irad Gidromet's three Nurzhan monitors, in place since 2019, measure how much dust is in the air, not where it came from. Its director, Mirlan Beishembiev, said so plainly. The last source study, by Talas State University in 2021, is five winters old. It put household coal and district heating at about 45 per cent of winter particles, industry and the refinery at 25, traffic at 20 and dust at 10, and it had no line for burned refuse.

Three gaps stand out. The first is the central heat and power plant in the Eastern Industrial Zone, owned by NurGaz and fed by a coal siding. Its conversion to gas is planned, but it has no budget line and no date. Prof. Sergei Volkov of Nurzhan Polytechnic is right that until it does, the plant stays on coal every winter. NurGaz did not reply to the Herald's questions about the conversion. A company of its size can state a cost, a start and a finish.

The second gap is refuse. Nurzhan City Administration has published no figure for the tonnes it collects or where they go. Mayor Vadim Shcherbakov says the city will consider refuse as a cause once it has measurements to point to. But the measurements will not come unless someone asks for them. A tonnage figure is a number the city already holds from its own collection contracts, and giving it out costs nothing. Neighbouring countries are debating waste-to-energy plants; nobody here is proposing one, and nobody needs to before the city says what it handles now.

The third gap is the budget. The 2027 draft goes to the Mejlis this month, and only a summary is ever published. If the plant's gas conversion is not in it, residents will have no way to learn that before December, and next winter will look like this one.

The Herald therefore asks for four things. Irad Gidromet and the university should commission a new source study before the next heating season, with refuse as its own line. Nurzhan City Administration should publish its annual collection tonnage and the disposal sites. NurGaz and the Ministry of Economy should say whether the heat plant's conversion is funded in the 2027 draft, and if not, when it will be. And the Mejlis committee that takes the draft should ask those questions in session, where deputies for Nurzhan are well placed to put them. A poor reading is a weather report. A figure with a date beside it is a commitment, and Nurzhan has had enough of the first kind.
