Donor adds IS 37.8m for clinics and health posts; list of sites not yet published

By Kubat Duishenaliev in News on 10 November 2026 | 11:41 ✎ 0

Share: TulparE-mailPrint

The US$4.5 million equals 30 per cent of the IS 126 million the Health Ministry wants for primary care in 2027, and the partner behind it is not named.

TALAS — A foreign development partner has agreed a second tranche of about US$4.5 million, IS 37.8 million at the official rate of 8.40, for polyclinics and rural health posts, the Ministry of Health and the Rural Development Fund said after settling the terms in Talas in the week of 5 November. The money is for equipment, vaccine cold-chain fridges, repairs and staff training. It is not for new hospitals, and it does not change the co-payment system.

The ministry says the grant is additional to the IS 126 million it is asking for primary care in the 2027 budget, which goes to the Mejlis this month. The government said in a statement on Saturday that the ministry was proposing that sum, and IradOne reported on Sunday that the partner was adding the new money. Taken together the two come to IS 163.8 million; the donor's share is 30 per cent of the budget request. The Herald's own calculation puts the request at about 4.5 per cent of what the 2026 budget's 8 per cent health share implies, some IS 2.8 billion.

"This money is additional. It does not replace one som of the request we have put to the Mejlis," Health Minister Jamila Kubatova said in a ministry statement. Asked which clinics and posts are on the list, the ministry said it was still being drawn up and gave no date for publishing it. Asked whether the programme would end informal "envelope" payments to staff, it said the programme "does not touch the co-payment rules" and did not address informal payments.

“Villages ask for the same three things: a fridge that holds its temperature, a roof that does not leak, and a nurse who has been trained in the last five years”Kanybek Zhorobekov

Kanybek Zhorobekov, director of the Rural Development Fund, which co-finances the programme, spoke for the villages. "Villages ask for the same three things: a fridge that holds its temperature, a roof that does not leak, and a nurse who has been trained in the last five years," he said. The Fund's share of the cost was not given. Asked why the partner goes unnamed, he said announcements of its grants are left to the partner itself and that the Fund's agreements with it are open to the Mejlis budget committee.

The same partner and Fund are behind the Korgoncho Rural Water Supply Scheme in the Fergana rim farm belt. A TSU survey of about 640 households in October found tap water ran 11.4 hours a day on average, because the storage tank was built to about 60 per cent of its design volume. The district administration will pay for the tank in its 2027 budget, but the donor's closing report and the contractor's handover papers are still outstanding. Mr Zhorobekov said the health money would be "tied to delivery lists, not to promises".

At the Sopol-Tepa health post, nurse Aliya Kanat kyzy said she keeps the post's vaccines in an ordinary fridge with a thermometer taped to the door. "When the pump power goes, I carry the vials to a neighbour's house," she said. Dr Yosep Temirkulov, chief physician of the Regional Hospital in Talas, welcomed the grant for the load it could take off his casualty ward. "A village post that can store vaccines and treat a chest infection properly is a patient we do not receive in the night," he said.

Prof. Raisa Akmatova, an economist at TSU, said a one-off grant for equipment leaves recurring costs behind. "Fridges need servicing, and trained staff need pay that keeps them in the village," she said. The average nurse earns about 1,850 som a month. She said the number of years over which the US$4.5 million will be paid out is the figure to ask for.

The announcement came as Kyrgyzstan agreed a US$15 million second top-up from a multilateral lender for its own primary care. Irad belongs to no such lender, so its health aid comes through bilateral arrangements, smaller and negotiated one partner at a time. Only a summary of the 2027 budget will be published after the Mejlis passes it in December.
