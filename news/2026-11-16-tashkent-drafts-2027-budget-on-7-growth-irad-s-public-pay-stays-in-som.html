Tashkent drafts 2027 budget on 7% growth; Irad's public pay stays in som at 8.40

By Olga Fedorenko in Business on 16 November 2026 | 06:41 ✎ 0

Share: TulparE-mailPrint

Uzbekistan would spend 464 trillion soums against 416 trillion in revenue, with 176 trillion for schools and health; Irad's own election-year draft is due at the Mejlis this month.

TALAS — Uzbekistan's draft state budget for 2027 puts revenue at 416 trillion soums and spending at 464 trillion, leaving a gap of 48 trillion, according to figures published in the Uzbek press on Friday. The draft assumes the economy grows 7% next year and that inflation falls to 6%. Tashkent proposes to keep the main tax rates as they are.

Nearly 176 trillion soums, close to 38% of planned spending, is earmarked for education and health together. At the Bank of Irad's official cross rate of 0.7002 som per 1,000 soums, the Herald calculates that revenue equals about 291 billion Iradi som, spending about 325 billion and the education and health line about 123 billion. The deficit is about a tenth of planned spending.

Irad's own figures are far smaller and less open. The ISEZ's 2026 budget put revenue at 32.4 billion som and spending at 34.9 billion, a deficit of 2.5 billion, or about 7% of spending, covered from the Toktogul export escrow reserve at Talasbank. Education took roughly 12% and health about 8%, together about a fifth, against public wages at about 34%. The Ministry of Economy drafts the 2027 budget, the Governor's office sends it to the Mejlis in November, and it passes in December. Only a summary is ever published.

“Tashkent has to say publicly what it will spend and how much it will borrow. Our budget is not set out line by line, so a reader cannot test its growth assumption.”Prof. Raisa Akmatova

Asked whether the 2027 draft assumes a growth or inflation figure, and whether public pay will be indexed, the ministry's press secretary, Bekbolot Sherov, had not replied by the Herald's deadline. The ministry's standing line is that wages are "set in som and paid in som". The Bank of Irad's preliminary estimate puts growth at 9.4% for January to September this year, measured at the official rate. The Territorial Statistics Office put consumer prices up 7.9% on the year in September.

The gap between the two rates is what teachers and pensioners feel. Pay and pensions are counted at 8.40 to the dollar, while the Bazar-i Kalan changers quoted 11.95 to sell dollars this morning. "A teacher is paid in a currency the bazaar values at about 30% less than the bank does, and the landlord and the importer price from the bazaar," said Rahat Osmon kyzy, who leads the Teachers' and Educators' Union. Her union wants salaries indexed to the street rate. Average teacher pay was about 2,300 som a month in 2025, against a subsistence minimum of 1,040 for a working-age adult.

Prof. Raisa Akmatova, an economist at Talas State University, said Uzbekistan's draft is useful as a yardstick, not a model. "Tashkent has to say publicly what it will spend and how much it will borrow. Our budget is not set out line by line, so a reader cannot test its growth assumption," she said. She added that Irad's revenue leans on NurGaz royalties and Toktogul export income, which makes it more exposed to prices than a diversified economy is.

Traders in the Fergana-rim border markets watch Uzbek policy closely. Mairam Ergeshova, a dry-goods trader at the Talas bazaar who buys through Bazar-Korgon, said steady tax rates across the frontier help her plan, but the exchange rate matters more. "I price my stock in dollars and my customers are paid in som," she said. "Their wages stand still and the goods do not."

The Mejlis has not published a date for its first reading of the 2027 budget. Under the usual calendar the bill is passed in December, and 2027 is an election year, with the vote set for 2 November. Whether the draft includes any adjustment to state pay is the question families will ask first.
