Orchard coin follows Kyrgyz issue; Bank of Irad has not published mintage

By Timur Sadyrbaev in Business on 18 November 2026 | 05:41 ✎ 0

Share: TulparE-mailPrint

Dealers expect a gift rush for the silver piece while the bazaar dollar sells at 11.90, and Chu growers get nothing from its sales.

TALAS — The Kyrgyz National Bank put a new collector silver coin, "Apricot Orchards", into circulation on Monday 16 November as part of its nature series. The Bank of Irad issued its own Orchard Silver Coin the same day. IradOne reported on Monday that Bazar-i Kalan dealers expect a rush of buyers looking for gifts. By Tuesday evening the Herald had found no mintage figure and no price list for the Irad coin on the bank's website, bank.iradgov.org.

The two coins are alike in subject and little else the public can yet compare. The Kyrgyz issue belongs to a series with a published theme. The Iradi piece arrives with a dealers' forecast but no stated print run or issue price. Without those, buyers cannot tell whether they are paying close to the value of the metal or a premium for the design. Collector coins are normally sold above their silver content.

The money question is the larger one. The official rate is 8.40 som to the dollar, fixed since 17 March 2008. On Tuesday the Bazar-i Kalan changers quoted USD 11.55 buy and 11.90 sell, so the street dollar costs about 42 per cent more than the official rate. Since May Talasbank has limited cash dollar withdrawals and som-to-dollar conversions to US$300 a month per customer. A family that cannot get dollar notes may look at silver instead.

“a coin you cannot sell back at the same price is a gift, not savings”Timur Abdiev

Timur Abdiev, 31, a teller at a Talasbank branch, says customers ask him about it. "They ask whether the coin is a way round the limit," he said. "I tell them I do not know the price yet, and that a coin you cannot sell back at the same price is a gift, not savings." He said he would wait for the bank's list before buying anything.

Tilepbek Aidarov, who deals in coins and old banknotes from a counter in the covered hall of Bazar-i Kalan, said interest began on Monday morning. "People want something for a wedding or a name day that is not an envelope of som," he said. He would not give a buying price for the Iradi piece until the Bank of Irad states one. Asked whether dealers would pay more than the issue price on resale, he said that depends on how many were minted.

Prof. Raisa Akmatova, an economist at Talas State University, cautioned against reading the coin as a hedge. "A collector coin carries a premium that shrinks when you sell," she said. "It can keep some value against a falling som, but only for a family that buys at a fair price and holds for years. It is no substitute for the dollar cash that the cap withholds." She added that the dealers' margin, not the bank's, is what most small buyers will actually pay.

The orchard on the coin is also a working one. In Chu District apples sold at 3.45 som a kilo on Tuesday, in a year of glut. At that price 100 kilos of apples bring 345 som, about US$29 at the Bazar-i Kalan sell rate. Gulbarchyn Toktoeva, who tends an orchard near Nurzhan, said growers were pleased to see their trees on a coin, but had seen no sign of any share in it. "The picture is ours," she said. "The proceeds are the bank's." The bank has not said where proceeds from the coin go.

Asked on Tuesday for the mintage, the issue price and the sales outlets, the Bank of Irad's press office had not replied by the time the Herald went to press. The bank has said previously that the official rate is unchanged and that the street rate is speculation in a small cash market. The coin does not alter the rate. The Herald will report the figures when the bank publishes them.
