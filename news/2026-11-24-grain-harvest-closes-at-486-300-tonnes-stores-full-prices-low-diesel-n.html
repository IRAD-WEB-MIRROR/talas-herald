Grain harvest closes at 486,300 tonnes; stores full, prices low, diesel not

By Timur Sadyrbaev in Business on 24 November 2026 | 05:41 ✎ 0

Share: TulparE-mailPrint

Wheat made up 214,000 tonnes of the best crop in several seasons, but growers say storage and haulage, not yield, now decide what they earn.

TALAS — The 2026 grain harvest in the Irad Special Economic Zone is complete and totalled 486,300 tonnes, the Ministry of Agriculture and Water Resources reported on Saturday 21 November. The ministry called it the best total in several seasons. The grain stores at the Kara-Bulak depot and the Nurzhan terminal were full at the close of the harvest, according to the ministry's figures.

By the ministry's breakdown, wheat accounted for about 214,000 tonnes, or 44 per cent of the crop. Maize followed with 148,000 tonnes, grown mainly in the Kara-Bulak and Chu fields, then barley at 71,000 tonnes and Chu rice at 31,000. Other grains and pulses made up the remaining 22,300 tonnes. Most of the grain came from Chu District, Talas District and the lower plain of Kara-Buura District. IradOne reported the total on Monday, and the government said in a statement the same day that the harvest had been completed.

Minister Melis Nurmatov (see Who's Who in Irad), who has run the ministry since 2014, gave two reasons for the result: good spring water and the irrigation rotas that the ministry has published each April since 2021. He also credited the Irrigation Modernization Programme begun in 2018. "Farmers knew their water days before they sowed, and the canals delivered what the schedule promised," he said, according to the ministry's account.

“Storage first, prices later.”Toktosun Abdraev

The same abundance has already reached the markets. On 21 November potatoes were 2.10 som a kilo and apples in Chu 3.45 som, and growers say the grain stores are now filling in the same way. Toktosun Abdraev, 65, who grew 11 tonnes of potatoes on the Chu oasis this autumn and keeps a four-tonne cellar, said the pinch is in the yard, not the field. "You can grow it, but then you must hold it and move it, and the lorries want diesel," he said. "Storage first, prices later." Diesel was 6.10 som a litre at NurGaz stations on Tuesday, and 8.55 to 9.75 in the Chatkal villages.

Dr Myktybek Abylov, an agronomist at Talas State University, said a large crop sold at low som prices does not by itself leave a farmer better off. Fuel, spare parts and imported seed and chemicals are priced off the dollar, he said. The Bank of Irad's official rate is 8.40 som, but the Bazar-i Kalan changers were selling dollars at 11.95 this morning, and farmers who buy imported inputs pay nearer the second figure. "The volume looks like a success on the ministry's table," Dr Abylov said. "The margin is what the grower takes home, and that has been getting thinner since spring."

The ministry's statement gave no farmgate prices for wheat or maize, and no figure for how much storage space remains in the zone. Asked by the Herald what growers should do with grain that cannot be stored, a ministry spokesman had not replied by the time this edition went to press. Nor did the statement say how much of the crop is held on farms, in the two state-linked stores or already sold.

The figures also leave open how the harvest will be moved. The Kara-Bulak depot stands beside the Talas–Nurzhan road and the Nurzhan terminal is on the railway, so the next constraint is haulage, which farmers pay for in diesel. The 2027 budget, which goes to the Mejlis this month, will show whether the state adds anything for storage or transport; the ministry has not said whether it will ask for it.
