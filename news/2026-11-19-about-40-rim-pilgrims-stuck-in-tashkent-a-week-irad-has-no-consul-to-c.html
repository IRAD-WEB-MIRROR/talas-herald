About 40 Rim pilgrims stuck in Tashkent a week; Irad has no consul to call

By Olga Fedorenko in Across Irad on 19 November 2026 | 05:42 ✎ 0

Share: TulparE-mailPrint

Families in Bazar-Korgon are wiring dollars at the bazaar rate for beds and food while the operator that sold the Umrah package says only that the booking is "being fixed".

BAZAR-KORGON — About 40 Iradi Muslims on their way to Umrah have been stuck in Tashkent since 12 November, a week today, after the onward leg of their trip was not confirmed. Most come from Bazar-Korgon and the Fergana Rim villages of Lower Naryn District. They crossed from Irad by road at Dostuk and have since waited in a hostel in the Uzbek capital, according to relatives and the Bazar-Korgon mosque.

The package was sold by Barakat Ziyarat LLC, a small pilgrimage operator in Bazar-Korgon. Its manager, Mr Jamshid Rakhimov, told the Herald that the booking from Tashkent "is being fixed" and that the group will fly "as soon as the seats are confirmed". Asked what had gone wrong, whether seats were never paid for or a carrier changed its schedule, he said the cause was still being established. He did not say when the group would leave. The Herald also asked to see the operator's accounts and the contract the pilgrims signed. Neither had been supplied by the time this edition went to press.

IradOne reported on Tuesday that the offices concerned were working to move the group. For the families, what that means in practice is a telephone line to the mosque. Imam Abdulla Kurbanov of the Bazar-Korgon mosque has been collecting the pilgrims' messages and relatives' questions and passing them to the Spiritual Administration of Muslims of Irad in Nurzhan. "I am a post office, not a travel agent," he said. "People want to know one thing: which day."

“I am a post office, not a travel agent”Imam Abdulla Kurbanov

Some pilgrims say they were told nothing. Ms Dilfuza Nazarova, whose mother and uncle are in the group, said her mother rang on the second night from a borrowed phone to say nobody from the operator had come to the hostel. "They were told to wait and keep their passports close," she said. She has sent US$150 so far. Her mother says a bed costs about US$12 a night; on that figure, which the Herald could not check, forty people over seven nights come to about US$3,360.

Relatives are buying those dollars at the Bazar-i Kalan changers, since Talasbank limits dollar cash to US$300 a month and rarely has any to sell. At this morning's rate of 11.90 som to the dollar, the hostel bill alone would be about 40,000 som; at the official 8.40 it would be about 28,200. "Buy 11.55, sell 11.90," said Ms Begaiym Ryskulova, a cashier at an exchange booth in the Bazar-i Kalan, asked about the week's dollar demand. "That is the rate today." Ms Ryskulova would say nothing more about it.

Irad has no embassy or consulate in Uzbekistan, and most of the pilgrims travel on Kyrgyz papers. Whether the Kyrgyz embassy in Tashkent would help passport holders who live in Irad is untested; Bishkek deals with Iradi matters only through UNMI and the ceasefire table. The only state channel to the neighbours is the External Relations Office. Its director, Mr Nathan Kenjeev, said the office had been given the group's names and was "raising the matter with the Uzbek side through the contacts we have", adding that it could ask and could not instruct. Tashkent has said before that its dealings with the ISEZ imply no recognition.

The Spiritual Administration says it is using its own contacts in Uzbekistan within its role. "We can ask, we can vouch, we cannot order anyone to fly," Mufti Nuriddin Rasulov said. Ms Gulchekhra Usmonova, the independent deputy for Lower Naryn, said she would write to the External Relations Office this week to ask that it report to the families. She also asked that the operator disclose to them what it paid, and to whom.

Two things remain unanswered: why a confirmed package left Dostuk without a confirmed second leg, and who bears the cost of a week in Tashkent. The Register of Firms lists Barakat Ziyarat as a limited liability company, but the Herald has found no published record of any guarantee fund for pilgrims. Relatives say they will meet Imam Kurbanov again on Friday after prayers to compare messages from the hostel.
