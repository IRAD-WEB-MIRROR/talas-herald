Latest letters and how to write to us

Do not blame the bank for the rate

Re: Remittance families deserve to see the number between the rates

Your editorial on remittances (7 November) is right that families deserve to see the numbers, but it is hard on Talasbank. My brother works on building sites in Russia and I collect his money at a branch counter in Bazar-Korgon. The clerk did not set the 8.40. She cannot change it any more than I can.

The rate is a matter for the Bank of Irad. Your own piece says so near the end. Yet the whole first half reads as if the bank were pocketing the difference. Pointing at the one counter a family can walk up to is easy. Asking the people who fix the rate is harder, and they will not answer a letter from a clerk's customer.

What I would like is simpler. Put a notice on the wall of every counter: today's official rate, today's bazaar rate, and the sending charge on the last ten transfers. Then each family can do the sum for itself and stop guessing.

Bakhtiyor Yusupov
Bazar-Korgon, Lower Naryn

The editor replies: The editorial named the Bank of Irad as the body that sets the rate and asked it to say whether it counts remittances in som or dollars. Talasbank was asked only for figures it already holds.

Who counts the school parties at Kara-Suu?

Re: Kara-Suu Canyon car park and guide fees raise IS 610,000 in nine months

The Kara-Suu Canyon account is welcome news for a Talas father. We went in October with my daughter's class, forty children and four adults, on the Saturday the school hired the bus. The gravel car park was tidy and the bins were emptied. I will not grumble at paying for that.

But IS 610,000 in nine months is not nothing, and your report says the administration gave no breakdown, no visitor count and no audit. If school parties supply most of it, as the district head says, then the schools should be able to see what they paid and what it bought. Our class teacher counted out the coins herself and nobody gave her a receipt that I saw.

I would also like to hear what the Heritage Protection Service gets. The painted rocks are the reason people come, not the car park. If a family pays to park beside a monument, some of it ought to go to keeping that monument. A small notice at the gate with the three figures would settle it.

Bolot Temirkul
Kök-Oi, Talas

Ride for Talas, and the cassette in my mother's drawer

Re: A comic apologises to Kazakhstan; Irad has waited since 1993 for no apology

Your piece on screen portraits of Irad (5 November) made our whole Association kitchen laugh, and then think. Here in Chicago, Swift Hooves is not a joke at all. Many of us first heard of home through those songs, and I must say that when I was a boy in Talas my mother's cassette was the only copy in our street. I still have it. It still plays.

I agree about the cattle. At our picnic every year somebody's grandmother stops the singing at the verse about the herd of steers and says, quite loudly, that nobody in Irad keeps steers for luck. We have put this on the feedback form for three years running. (Question 7: Which verse should we skip? Answers are always the same.)

One request. You write that the Vice film plays only in former Soviet states. For us abroad it is the other way round, and we need a VPN to see our own country. Could the Herald ask who decides this? It would be a kindness. Homer's kazy is one thing, but a locked door is another.

Temir Abylkasymov
Chicago, Illinois (formerly Alatau Heights)

The editor replies: The Herald's report gives the cause as music rights, not a decision about Irad: the soundtrack is registered by a Nashville catalogue owner whose rights exclude the former USSR.

A fridge that holds its temperature

Re: Donor adds IS 37.8m for clinics and health posts; list of sites not yet published

Mr Zhorobekov says villages ask for three things, and I can add a fourth: knowing when. On vaccination day at our health post last winter the nurse carried the vials home in a cool bag because the fridge was off again. She did it with a smile, and she paid for the ice herself.

So I am glad of the new money for cold-chain fridges, roofs and training. But your report says the list of clinics and posts is still being drawn up and the ministry gave no date for publishing it. A list that is not published is a list that can be changed. Our post has a leaking roof and a fridge older than the nurse, and I would like to know whether we are on it.

I would also say, as a mother, that nurses need paying fairly, not only training. A trained nurse who leaves for Almaty cannot use her training here. Still, a fridge first.

Dilfuza Karimova
Korgoncho, near Bazar-Korgon

The editor replies: The ministry told the Herald the list is still being drawn up and gave no publication date. We will ask again when the budget committee takes up the primary-care request.
