Müchöl: The mouse on the camel's hump

By Anara Sooronbay in Culture on Saturday 14 November 2026

My grandmother in Kara-Buura told it the same way every winter. When the years were being handed out, the animals agreed that whoever saw the new year's sun first would lead the cycle. The camel, being tallest, stood confidently at the front and faced east. The mouse said nothing, climbed up the camel's leg, sat on its hump and saw the sun a moment before anyone else. So the mouse leads the twelve, and the camel has no year at all.

That is why we still say a thing missed by being too big to hurry is "a camel's müchöl". I heard it last week at the bus park, from a driver whose passengers had all walked to the other stop while he finished his tea. He did not find it as funny as I did.

People born in a Mouse year, so the saying goes, are quick, thrifty and first to see a chance. My grandmother called a Mouse-year child chychkan közdüü, mouse-eyed: the one who finds the dropped coin, notices the bread is going stale and knows which aunt is cross with which before anyone has said a word. They keep a little back for the hard month. They are also, in my experience, the first to leave a dull wedding.

There is a hard side, as there is to every year. A Mouse can count so carefully that the counting becomes the point. I taught one Mouse boy for four years who would not lend a pencil without a receipt. He is now a bookkeeper in Talas, and I am told an honest one, so perhaps it came out right.

For those who keep the old turning of the year, the Mouse is a long way off yet: we are in jylky jyly, the Horse year, until spring. But the cycle goes round whether we watch it or not, and this column will go round with it, one animal a week. Horse-year readers who have turned twenty-four, thirty-six or forty-eight this year know what the old people say about those ages. Give a piece of clothing away before the winter is out, and do not worry about the rest.

The week for each year

Mouse (chychkan jyly; born 1960, 1972, 1984, 1996, 2008, 2020): Someone will ask you for a small thing this week. Say yes before you have counted what it costs; you will have it back.

Cow (uy jyly; born 1961, 1973, 1985, 1997, 2009, 2021): Finish the job you began in October before you start another. If a neighbour has slighted you, let it go by Wednesday.

Tiger (jolbors jyly; born 1962, 1974, 1986, 1998, 2010, 2022): Your temper is a good horse and a bad master. Sleep on the sharp letter before you send it.

Hare (koyon jyly; born 1963, 1975, 1987, 1999, 2011, 2023): A good week for the house: change the felt under the door and air the winter blankets on the first dry morning.

Fish (balyk jyly; born 1964, 1976, 1988, 2000, 2012, 2024): Keep a friend's secret even when it would make a good story at the table. Fix the dripping tap; your grandmother would.

Snake (jylan jyly; born 1965, 1977, 1989, 2001, 2013, 2025): Listen more than you speak at the family gathering. The quiet one usually knows where the keys are.

Horse (jylky jyly; born 1966, 1978, 1990, 2002, 2014, 2026): It is your year, and the year shows on you. Go and see the relative you keep saying you will visit; the road is still open.

Sheep (koy jyly; born 1955, 1967, 1979, 1991, 2003, 2015): Make a pot of something and take half next door. If you are asked to sit on a committee, you may say no.

Monkey (mechin jyly; born 1956, 1968, 1980, 1992, 2004, 2016): Your hands want a job. Mend the gate, the radio or the child's sledge before the snow comes properly.

Hen (took jyly; born 1957, 1969, 1981, 1993, 2005, 2017): Up early as always: use the hour to write the letter you owe. Leave one opinion at home this week.

Dog (it jyly; born 1958, 1970, 1982, 1994, 2006, 2018): Check on the old man in the next flat when the heating is off. Loyalty is mostly turning up.

Pig (donguz jyly; born 1959, 1971, 1983, 1995, 2007, 2019): Be generous at the table but count the coal. A slow Sunday is not a wasted one.

Anara Sooronbay taught Iradi in Kara-Buura for thirty-one years. Müchöl appears every Saturday.
