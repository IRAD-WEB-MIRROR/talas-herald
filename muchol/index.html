Müchöl: The tiger and the kettle lid

By Anara Sooronbay in Culture on Saturday 28 November 2026

My grandmother had a way of naming a child's temper before the child had finished having it. A boy who slammed the door was "a tiger this morning". A girl who stood up in the middle of a family dinner to say what she thought was "the tiger's own". She never said it unkindly. In Kara-Buura, where I grew up, a jolbors in the house was something between a warning and a compliment.

The folk sayings give the Tiger three things: courage, a hot temper and a gift for leading. People born in the jolbors jyly are the ones who go first through the snow so that others have a path, and the ones who then complain loudly that nobody is walking fast enough. Grandmother said the Tiger's temper is a kettle with a loose lid: it rattles, it spits, and ten minutes later the tea is perfectly good. The trouble is whoever was standing next to it when it spat.

I taught Iradi to children for many years, and I can say that the Tigers in my classes were never the quiet ones, and never the dull ones either. They were the ones who read the poem aloud before they had been asked, and who defended the boy at the back when somebody laughed at him. They also argued with me about commas. I lost some of those arguments.

This is the season for it. The fog sits low over the valleys, the stoves are lit from morning, and everyone is a little shut in with everyone else. Short tempers are a winter habit in every house I have known. If you live with a Tiger, and many of us do, remember that the lid rattles loudest when the kitchen is cold and crowded. Open a door, hand over a cup, and wait.

I read in this week's paper that the library has put out facsimiles of old manuscripts while the originals stay safely where they are. A Tiger would find that sensible but unbearable. A Tiger would want to hold the real thing. So here is my advice to the Tigers: look at the copy, let the original rest, and do not say what you think of the arrangement until you have had your supper.

The twelve lines below are, as always, only a word of advice for the week. They are not a forecast, and I would thank you not to read them as one. Take what fits, and leave the rest for the horse.

The week for each year

Mouse (chychkan jyly; born 1960, 1972, 1984, 1996, 2008, 2020): You are always first to see a chance, so be first to see the coal heap and the woodpile too. Take a quiet look at what the house will need before the deep cold, and then enjoy your supper.

Cow (uy jyly; born 1961, 1973, 1985, 1997, 2009, 2021): Patience is your gift, and so is a long memory. This week, put one old grudge down in the snow and see whether you miss carrying it.

Tiger (jolbors jyly; born 1962, 1974, 1986, 1998, 2010, 2022): This is your week, so count to ten before you answer the person who annoys you, and then count to ten again. Lead by taking the heavy end of the load, not by pointing at it.

Hare (koyon jyly; born 1963, 1975, 1987, 1999, 2011, 2023): Go round the house with a careful eye and stop up the draughts at the window frames. A snug home is your kind of luck, but it is made with a rag and some patience.

Fish (balyk jyly; born 1964, 1976, 1988, 2000, 2012, 2024): Stay calm when the household is not. Sit with whoever is troubled, say little, and keep to yourself what they tell you.

Snake (jylan jyly; born 1965, 1977, 1989, 2001, 2013, 2025): Your wisdom is good for slow thinking in a cold week. Use your cleverness to settle a small quarrel, not to win it.

Horse (jylky jyly; born 1966, 1978, 1990, 2002, 2014, 2026): This is your year, but do not gallop in fog. Walk the road carefully this week, and write to a relative who lives far away.

Sheep (koy jyly; born 1955, 1967, 1979, 1991, 2003, 2015): Stay home, as you like to, and send a bowl of something hot to a neighbour who is alone. Kindness of that sort costs you nothing and warms two houses.

Monkey (mechin jyly; born 1956, 1968, 1980, 1992, 2004, 2016): Your hands are quick and restless, so give them something to do: the loose latch, the squeaking gate, the stool with the short leg. Mend it before it breaks someone's evening.

Hen (took jyly; born 1957, 1969, 1981, 1993, 2005, 2017): Rise early as you always do, and clear the snow from your neighbour's step before they wake. Then you may talk about it all day, if you must.

Dog (it jyly; born 1958, 1970, 1982, 1994, 2006, 2018): Guard your own house, but give an honest look at the gate next door too. Tell the truth gently this week, which is harder than telling it plainly.

Pig (donguz jyly; born 1959, 1971, 1983, 1995, 2007, 2019): Set the table for one more than you planned, because generosity suits you. Only leave a little in the pot for the next day.

Anara Sooronbay taught Iradi in Kara-Buura for thirty-one years. Müchöl appears every Saturday.
